What Letter Is This?

Photograph the Korean paper that came in the post. It tells you what kind of letter it is, by when, how much and what you have to do — and opens the number to call. Korean and English. For Android phones.

Getting ready…

Open this page on the phone and press the button. If you download it on a computer you will have to move the file across.

The photo never leaves your phone

  • The text reader is built into the app. It works with no internet at all — in airplane mode, and on phones with no Google Play.
  • The paper is read on the phone. It is sent nowhere.
  • No account, no server, no monthly fee, and not one line of anybody else's advertising.

Once you have it

  1. Tap the downloaded file (Valuestools What Letter.apk).
  2. If it asks about "apps from unknown sources", choose allow. — that is because it is not on the Play Store yet.
  3. Open the app and press Photograph the letter.
  4. In a moment you get the five boxes below. Tap the phone-number line and the dialler opens.

What you get — five boxes

What kind of letter Stay extension · workplace change · fine · health insurance …
By when The deadline, big, at the top. If the paper does not say, it does not invent one
How much The amount exactly as printed
What to do Short lines, one task each
Who to call 1345 · 1350 · 1577-1366 … tap and it dials

Every phone number was checked on the agency's own official site on 5 October 2026. None of them is made up. 1345 answers in 20 languages; 1577-1366 in 15.

What it does not do — written down plainly

This is not legal advice. It reads the paper and points out what kind of letter it is and what has to be done by when. For anything that matters, call the number it shows and ask a person.

The less text the camera catches, the less it gets right. Measured on real public notices: with about 1,200 characters it was right 84.3% of the time; with only 350 characters, 55.7%. Photograph the whole page in good light.

If the paper has no deadline, it does not invent one. When it does not know, it says so.

Nobody has yet photographed a real letter with a real phone. The screens, the install and the built-in reader have been checked; how well the camera reads an actual notice has not been measured. Try it and leave one line — the next build will fix what you find.

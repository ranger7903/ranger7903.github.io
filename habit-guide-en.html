🔁 One habit a week, not five at once

Start five changes on the same New Year’s Day and by day three all five fall together. Write down the things you want to change, then give your attention to one per week and go round in a loop.

What the real problem is

Up early, exercise, read, drink less, lose my temper less — five things, same day, same time. On the evening of day three they collapse together. Once one fails, “today’s ruined anyway” takes the rest with it.

What Benjamin Franklin did

As a young man, Franklin wrote down thirteen virtues he wanted to build and gave his attention to one per week. Thirteen weeks made a round; four rounds a year. In a small notebook he drew seven day-columns and put a dot on each day he slipped.

The key: that week he watched only that one. Whether he broke the other twelve that week, he didn’t judge.

The one line

Try to keep ten at once and ten fall together. One a week, and if it falls, only one falls.

This is in Franklin’s own Autobiography — not a second-hand story. He also admitted openly that he never fully kept them all.

Five steps for tonight

1. Write each change as an action

✗ Lose my temper less ○ Don't interrupt — a dot each time I do ✗ Be more diligent ○ Empty the sink before bed ✗ Be better with my kid ○ Say “let's do it this way” instead of “no”

You need to be able to answer “did I or didn’t I?” in the evening.

2. Keep it between six and twelve

Six is a six-week round; twelve, twelve weeks. One round within three months is about right.

3. One per week — ignore the rest

This is the part people can’t do. If this week is “don’t interrupt,” whether you drank or skipped the gym is not this week’s business. No guilt. It sounds odd, but that is how this week’s one survives.

4. Mark the days you slip — not the days you succeed

This week: don't interrupt Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat Sun · ·· · ← four dots. It comes round again.

Empty squares are good. Marking only slips leaves nothing to second-guess.

5. After a round, go back to the start

You don’t have to fix it in one go — it comes back in two months. Not “I failed” but “four dots this round, let’s see next round.” That is why people don’t quit.

Three common mistakes

  • Minding the other ones too — then it is the old way again. That week, just one.
  • Calling a dotty week a failure — lots of dots means that one is the most urgent. You found something out.
  • Making the notebook pretty — draw lines on one sheet. Decorating becomes the job, and then you stop.

Eight to start from

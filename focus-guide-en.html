🎯 When you want to do too many things

Busy all the time with nothing to show for it? The cause is usually not a lack of time; it is holding too many things. Write down twenty-five, keep five, and move the other twenty from “later” to “not until the five are done.” One sheet of paper, thirty minutes.

What the real problem is

When busy-but-empty days pile up, we decide to manage time better: a new planner, a new app, an earlier alarm. But usually the problem isn’t time. Do fifteen things a little, and all fifteen get done only a little.

A well-known story — twenty-five to five

The story goes that Warren Buffett told his pilot: write down twenty-five things you want to do, circle the five that matter most, and treat the other twenty as a list you do not touch at all until the five are done.

To be honest about it

There is no good record of Buffett himself saying this; it spread as a story told by a pilot. The method works anyway — so below is how to do it, rather than who said it.

The heart of it is not picking five. It is moving the other twenty from “later” to “not now.” Things marked “later” keep taking up room in your head, and that drains you.

Five steps for tonight

1. Write all twenty-five (don’t trim)

Big, small, slightly embarrassing — all of it. Not “exercise” but “walk twice a week.” If you only get to fifteen, that’s fine. What stays in your head can’t be counted.

2. Sleep on it

Don’t choose the same day; that day’s mood will choose for you. The next morning, five or six will have wilted on their own.

3. Circle five — not “important” but “a waste not to do this year”

  • If not this year, will it be impossible next year? (a child’s age, a parent’s health, a deadline)
  • Does doing this make the other four easier? (money, health, a skill underneath everything)
  • A year from now, “glad I did” or “so what”?

4. Copy the rest onto a different sheet

━━━ Not to be touched until the five are finished ━━━ (Not thrown away. Just not now.)

On the same sheet they keep catching your eye. Making “not now” visible is a relief — nothing is forgotten, it’s written down.

5. Put one thing for this week next to each of the five

Get the certificate → this week: find the exam date, put it in the calendar (20 min) Trip for my parents → this week: ask Mum where she'd like to go (one call) Sort the flat → this week: one shelf of the shoe rack

When choosing is hard — compare two at a time

Hard to answerEasy to answer
Which of the twenty-five matters most?If only one of #1 and #2 could happen, which?
I want them all — what now?If one of these two had to go, which?
Can I drop this?This time next year, will I miss having done it?

Winners meet winners. Only two at a time, so your head doesn’t ache.

Three common mistakes

  • Choosing five “areas” — one each from work, health, family, money and hobbies picks your whole life, not five things. Leaning to one side is fine.
  • Keeping the twenty as “if I find time” — you won’t. Writing “not doing” is what makes it five.
  • Choosing once and never again — look every three months. When the five are done, take out the twenty.

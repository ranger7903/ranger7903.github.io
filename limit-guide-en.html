🧭 Why resolutions die on day three

Resolutions rarely last three days. The reason is not weak willpower; it is that the first day’s bar was set too high. Here is how to draw a line for “at least this much today” — and a second line for “however well it goes, I stop here.”

Why it collapses on day three

On the day you decide, you are full of energy, so you set the most you could do that day as the standard. The trouble is that the standard is still there tomorrow and the day after. Within two days it is no longer “the amount to do” — it is “the amount I failed to do.”

The person who quits on day three is not lazy. They quit because they started too well.

Jim Collins: the “stop doing” list

Jim Collins, author of Good to Great, has said in essays and talks that a stop-doing list matters more than a to-do list. Plenty of people write down what to add each year; few write down what they will not do.

A ceiling goes one step further: since choosing what to stop is hard, draw the line “only this far today” first.

The one line

Change today’s goal from “how much” to “where do I stop?”

Four steps, starting tonight

1. Set the floor by your worst day, not your best

The day your body is heaviest, the day of the work dinner, the day a child is ill — what you can do even then is the floor. For reading, not thirty pages a day but two.

Bad floor : 30 pages → busy day 0 → next day “catch up” 60 → give up Good floor: 2 pages → 2 even on a busy day → it continues

2. Draw a line at the top as well — this is the real ceiling

Most people only set a minimum. Set a maximum too.

Today's minimum: 2 pages Today's maximum: 10 pages — close the book even if I want more

Binging on a good day feels great, but the next day your body remembers “as much as yesterday.” A ceiling is what you leave for tomorrow’s you.

3. Decide the stop signal in advance

  • An alarm at 10 pm → finish the line you are on and close it
  • A 25-minute timer → stop when it rings (stopping while you still want one more round is the point)
  • Decide the end point first: “up to this paragraph”

4. Write one line

Not how much you did, but whether you kept to the lines. A tick or a cross is enough.

28 Sep ✓ 2 pages (tired, minimum only) 29 Sep ✓ 8 pages 30 Sep ✗ 15 pages — too good to stop. Did nothing on 1 Oct

After two weeks your own ceiling becomes visible. It is different for everyone.

It works for work too

What you used to doWith a ceiling
Finish the whole report todayToday, the outline only. The body tomorrow
Answer every emailOpen the inbox twice: 20 minutes morning, 20 afternoon
Answer every message at onceUnless urgent, batch them at set times
Catch up at the weekendWeekend set to zero — that is a line too

Three common mistakes

  • Setting the line too high — it should feel almost too easy. Easy is what continues.
  • Making up for missed days — don’t move yesterday’s share to today. Leave yesterday as a cross.
  • Starting several at once — put a ceiling on one thing at a time.

One more thing

You may have heard that “willpower runs out as you use it.” When large teams tried to repeat the famous experiments, the effect did not reappear (big replication studies in 2016 and 2021). So don’t blame weak willpower. Far more often, the bar was simply too high.

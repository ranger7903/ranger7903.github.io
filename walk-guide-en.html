🚶 Busy all day, thought about nothing

Busy all day and thought about nothing? It isn’t lack of time — the empty moments where we used to drift have all been filled by screens. Here is how to put twenty minutes into your calendar instead.

What the real problem is

A minute at the lights, twenty seconds in the lift, the bathroom, the bus — the idle spots we used to have are gone. Yet thinking usually happens in exactly those spots. Sit at a desk and say “right, think” and nothing comes.

Sara Blakely’s “fake commute”

Sara Blakely, who founded Spanx, lives a few minutes from her office, so she deliberately drives the long way round in the morning. She has said her best ideas come in the car — so she invented a commute she didn’t have.

The one line

Thinking time doesn’t arrive in your spare time, because there is no spare time. It has to be in the calendar.

No car needed. What matters is twenty minutes of moving without a screen.

Four steps, from today

1. Decide where the twenty minutes come from

  • Leave twenty minutes early and get off one stop sooner
  • After lunch, leave the phone on the desk and walk
  • One loop of the block before going home
  • Sit for twenty minutes before the family wakes
  • Washing up or folding laundry with nothing playing (the easiest)

2. Nothing in your ears either

This is the part people skip. With music, radio or a podcast, your head stays full even without a screen. The first two or three days may feel unbearably dull. That dullness is where thoughts come up. Hold on for three days.

3. Take one thing to think about

✗ “I'll have a think” → worry the whole way, come back ○ “What takes the most effort this month for the least return?” ○ “My kid's gone quiet — what haven't I asked?” ○ “What should I say first to them next week?”

One sentence. If your mind wanders off, let it — wandering isn’t bad.

4. Come back and write one line

28 Sep Repacking eats all my time. Fold the envelopes in advance? 29 Sep (nothing much) 30 Sep Ask “who did you play with today?” instead of “how was school?”

“Nothing much” on half the days is normal. Two or three lines in ten days is doing very well.

How this differs from resting

RestingThese twenty minutes
Lying down with a screenMoving without a screen
Filling your headEmptying it
Afterwards, time has goneAfterwards, one line is left
Done when there’s timePut in the calendar

Resting isn’t bad. They are different things and you need both — but rest can’t stand in for thinking time.

Three common mistakes

  • Making it an hour — you won’t keep an hour. Twenty minutes lasts three months; it grows longer by itself.
  • Expecting a good idea — seven times out of ten nothing comes. You go out for the day it does.
  • Taking the phone “but not looking” — if it’s with you, you’ll look. Leave it, or bury it deep in a bag.
